<b>Q: How long do I have to renew after a trademark filing expires?</b>

**A:** For a **U.S. federal trademark registration**, the USPTO provides a **six-month grace period after the registration's expiration/renewal deadline**. You can still file the required **Sections 8 and 9 combined maintenance/renewal filing** during those six months, but additional fees apply. ([USPTO][1])

The basic timeline is:

| Period                                | Status                                                         |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 9th–10th year after registration      | Normal renewal window                                          |
| First 6 months after 10th anniversary | **Grace period; renewal still permitted with surcharge**       |
| After the 6-month grace period        | Registration expires/cancels; ordinarily **cannot be revived** |

The USPTO specifically states that if the Sections 8/9 filing is not made before the grace period ends, the registration cannot simply be reinstated; generally, you must submit a **new trademark application**. ([USPTO][2])

One distinction matters: if by **"trademark filing expires"** you mean an **application was abandoned**, rather than an existing **registration reached its renewal deadline**, different rules apply. An abandoned application may sometimes be revived by petition. ([USPTO][3])
