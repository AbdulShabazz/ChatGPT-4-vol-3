**Q: Could the notion of gravity being carried along fibers satisfy the wave-particle duality phenomenon?**

**A:** **Potentially, but “fibers” alone do not explain wave–particle duality.** They could provide a substrate whose quantized excitations behave simultaneously as propagating waves and discrete interaction events.

Suppose gravity were transmitted by disturbances along microscopic fibers:

* A disturbance could propagate continuously along the fiber as a wave.
* Quantization of the fiber’s normal modes could produce discrete energy packets,
  [
  E=\hbar\omega
  ]
* Those packets could be emitted or absorbed as localized events, producing particle-like behavior.

This would resemble phonons: elastic waves in a crystal become particle-like quanta when the elastic field is quantized. A hypothetical gravitational fiber excitation could similarly function as a graviton-like quasiparticle.

However, this only reproduces the **generic mechanism of quantum wave–particle duality**. To reproduce gravity, the fiber theory would also need to yield:

1. A massless spin-2 excitation.
2. Universal coupling to energy and momentum.
3. The equivalence principle.
4. General relativity’s nonlinear field equations at macroscopic scales.
5. Gravitational waves traveling at the speed of light with the observed tensor polarizations.
6. No detectable preferred direction or stationary mechanical medium.
7. Lorentz invariance to extremely high precision.

A literal physical fiber network tends to introduce preferred directions, lattice dispersion, or a preferred rest frame. Those effects are strongly constrained experimentally. A dynamically reconfiguring, Lorentz-invariant, or quantum-entangled fiber network might avoid those problems, but it would require substantially more structure than ordinary mechanical fibers.

There is also a terminology distinction:

* **Fiber bundles in general relativity and gauge theory** are mathematical structures. Their fibers do not literally transport gravity.
* **Strings, spin networks, tensor networks, or causal graphs** can act more like the physical or informational fibers in your proposal.
* **Metric perturbations** already provide the conventional description: the gravitational wave is a distributed field configuration, while its hypothetical quantum is the graviton.

The strongest formulation would therefore be:

> Gravity is an emergent collective excitation of a microscopic fiber-like quantum network; the excitation propagates according to a wave equation but exchanges energy and momentum in quantized units.

That construction could satisfy wave–particle duality. It would not explain duality uniquely, because ordinary quantum field theory already predicts the same behavior without requiring an underlying fiber substrate.

**Q: What if the fibers connections exist through quantum entanglement and are free to pass through one another due to each existing in unique dimensions?**

**A:** **That formulation is mathematically more coherent than literal mechanical fibers.** The “fibers” would become **entanglement links in a pregeometric quantum network**, while ordinary three-dimensional space would be an emergent projection of that network.

## Crossing without interaction

Two fibers could appear to cross in three-dimensional space without interacting because their intersection would exist only in the projection. In the underlying network, they would be independent edges:

[
e_1=(A,B,\alpha), \qquad e_2=(C,D,\beta)
]

where (\alpha) and (\beta) identify distinct quantum channels. Unless the theory contains a coupling between (\alpha) and (\beta), there is no physical intersection.

A suitable Hilbert-space structure would be

[
\mathcal H
==========

\bigotimes_i
\bigotimes_\alpha
\mathcal H_i^{(\alpha)}.
]

Each “dimension” is then better interpreted as an **orthogonal internal degree of freedom**, not necessarily an additional spatial axis. This avoids requiring one literal universe-sized spatial dimension for every fiber.

This resembles spin-network and tensor-network approaches, where edges are abstract quantum relations rather than strands embedded in a pre-existing space. Spin networks, for example, are labeled graphs used to represent quantum-geometric states. ([arXiv][1])

## Role of entanglement

A fiber between nodes (i) and (j) might be represented by an entangled state

[
|\psi_{ij}^{(\alpha)}\rangle
\in
\mathcal H_i^{(\alpha)}
\otimes
\mathcal H_j^{(\alpha)}.
]

Its entanglement strength could contribute to emergent distance or geometric adjacency. A heuristic relation might be

[
d_{ij}
======

-\ell_0
\ln\left(\frac{I_{ij}}{I_0}\right),
]

where (I_{ij}) is mutual information. Strongly entangled nodes would appear geometrically close; weakly entangled nodes would appear distant.

This general direction has precedent: proposals by Van Raamsdonk relate spacetime connectivity to quantum entanglement, while the ER=EPR conjecture relates certain entangled systems to nontraversable Einstein–Rosen connections. These remain theoretical programs rather than experimentally established descriptions of ordinary spacetime. ([arXiv][2])

## Entanglement cannot itself carry gravity

The critical limitation is that **entanglement alone cannot transmit a controllable signal, energy, or force**. Entangled measurements are correlated, but local manipulation of one endpoint cannot communicate information instantaneously to the other. ([arXiv][3])

Therefore the model needs two distinct elements:

1. **Entanglement defines connectivity.**
2. **A Hamiltonian defines propagation.**

For example,

[
H
=

\sum_{(i,j,\alpha)}
J_{ij}^{(\alpha)}
O_i^{(\alpha)}
O_j^{(\alpha)}
+
H_{\mathrm{rewire}}.
]

The first term permits excitations to evolve along existing links. The second permits the connectivity or entanglement structure itself to change.

A gravitational wave would then be a propagating collective deformation of the network—not an instantaneous change transmitted through entanglement.

## Wave–particle duality

This framework could produce wave–particle duality naturally:

* A distributed excitation across many entanglement links behaves as a **wave**.
* Discrete excitation eigenstates or quantized energy transfers behave as **particles**.
* A localized detection event records one quantum of the collective mode.

Schematically,

[
|\Psi\rangle
============

\sum_e a_e |1_e\rangle
]

describes one quantum distributed over many fibers. It propagates like a wave through the amplitudes (a_e), but detection produces one discrete event.

The hypothetical graviton would therefore not be a tiny object traveling inside one fiber. It would be a quantized normal mode of the entire entanglement network.

## The “unique dimensions” issue

Assigning a completely separate physical dimension to every fiber is probably excessive. It creates:

* an enormous or unbounded dimensional space;
* no obvious mechanism selecting four macroscopic spacetime dimensions;
* difficult conservation laws;
* arbitrary fiber labels with little predictive content.

A cleaner interpretation is:

> Every fiber occupies a distinct orthogonal quantum channel, representation, or gauge sector, while all fibers jointly generate the emergent spacetime geometry.

The fibers can overlap in emergent space because they are not fundamentally located in emergent space.

## Required physical result

To become a gravity theory, the model must show that its low-energy collective excitation:

[
h_{\mu\nu}
]

behaves as a massless spin-2 field, couples universally to the stress-energy tensor,

[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}
\propto
h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu},
]

and reproduces the Einstein field equation,

[
G_{\mu\nu}
==========

8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}.
]

Research has shown relationships between entanglement conditions and Einstein-like equations in restricted settings, but this does not yet establish that gravity is literally an entanglement-fiber network. ([arXiv][4])

The strongest version of the proposal is therefore:

> Fundamental reality is a dynamically rewiring, multidimensional entanglement graph. Its links occupy orthogonal internal sectors and may cross freely in emergent space. Spacetime geometry reflects the graph’s entanglement structure, while gravity and gravitons are respectively classical and quantized collective deformations of that structure.

**Q: This sounds similar to Stephen Wolfram's physical model.**

**A:** Yes. **Your proposal is structurally close to the Wolfram Physics Project**, particularly its use of abstract connectivity rather than objects embedded in a pre-existing space. The important difference is that your formulation currently combines structures that Wolfram treats separately.

| Your concept                                   | Approximate Wolfram-model analogue                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fibers connecting fundamental elements         | Hyperedges connecting hypergraph nodes                                                        |
| Fibers cross without interacting               | Graph edges have no intrinsic geometric embedding; drawn crossings are physically meaningless |
| Each fiber occupies a unique dimension/channel | Hyperedges carry abstract relational identity rather than occupying ordinary space            |
| Entanglement establishes connectivity          | Branchial graphs encode relations and possible entanglement between quantum states            |
| Gravity propagates through the fiber network   | Gravity emerges from the large-scale causal structure generated by hypergraph rewriting       |
| Particle-like gravitational packets            | Localized stable hypergraph structures or quantized collective modes                          |

## Principal similarity

In the Wolfram model, fundamental space is represented by an evolving hypergraph,

[
\mathcal H_t=(V_t,E_t),
]

updated through local rewriting rules,

[
\mathcal H_t \longrightarrow \mathcal H_{t+1}.
]

The nodes do not initially possess coordinates, distances, or locations. Geometry is inferred from connectivity. Consequently, two hyperedges may cross in a drawing without intersecting physically; the crossing is merely an artifact of projecting an abstract graph onto a page or screen. ([arXiv][1])

That closely matches your assertion that fibers can pass through one another because their fundamental identity is not determined by ordinary three-dimensional position.

## Critical distinction: spatial versus branchial connectivity

Wolfram separates two major structures:

1. **Spatial hypergraph:** represents the instantaneous relational structure from which ordinary space may emerge.
2. **Multiway/branchial structure:** represents different possible computational histories or quantum states.

A branchial graph connects states that share ancestry in the multiway evolution. Wolfram interprets branchial proximity as potentially corresponding to quantum entanglement. ([Wolfram Physics Project][2])

Your proposal instead appears to say:

> The elementary spatial connections themselves are entanglement channels.

That is stronger. It identifies a hyperedge directly with an entangled relation, whereas the Wolfram model generally associates entanglement with relationships among branches or states of the whole rewriting system.

## Gravity

In Wolfram’s framework, gravity is not normally described as something transported along individual entanglement fibers. Instead:

* hypergraph updates generate causal relations;
* the causal graph defines emergent spacetime;
* curvature appears as nonuniform causal connectivity;
* under specified continuum assumptions, this structure is argued to approximate Einstein-like gravitational dynamics. ([arXiv][1])

Your model could be expressed differently:

[
G=(V,E,\rho),
]

where every edge (e_{ij}^{(\alpha)}) possesses an associated quantum state or density operator,

[
\rho_{ij}^{(\alpha)}
\in
\mathcal H_i^{(\alpha)}
\otimes
\mathcal H_j^{(\alpha)}.
]

The geometry would then depend not merely on whether an edge exists, but on its entanglement characteristics:

[
g_{\mu\nu}
==========

F!\left(
E,,
S_{ij},,
I_{ij},,
\phi_{ij}
\right),
]

where:

* (S_{ij}) is entanglement entropy;
* (I_{ij}) is mutual information;
* (\phi_{ij}) is a phase or channel state.

That produces an **entanglement-weighted hypergraph**, rather than Wolfram’s more purely combinatorial starting point.

## “Unique dimensions” and branchial space

Your unique-dimension proposal also resembles Wolfram’s concept of **branchial space**, but not ordinary extra spatial dimensions. Branchial directions represent distinctions among quantum branches rather than directions such as (x), (y), and (z).

A cleaner formulation would therefore replace:

> Every fiber exists in its own physical dimension.

with:

> Every fiber occupies an orthogonal relational or branchial channel in the underlying state space.

Mathematically,

[
\langle e_\alpha | e_\beta \rangle = 0,
\qquad \alpha\neq\beta.
]

Fibers can then overlap under projection into emergent spacetime while remaining distinguishable in the fundamental state space.

## Where your model diverges constructively

Your proposal adds three potentially meaningful features:

1. **Entanglement-native edges:** every fundamental connection is explicitly a quantum channel.
2. **Channel orthogonality:** overlapping connections remain independent because they occupy distinct internal sectors.
3. **Gravity as network excitation:** gravitational radiation becomes a propagating change in edge states or entanglement strength.

A possible local update law would be

[
\left(
E_t,\rho_t
\right)
\rightarrow
\left(
E_{t+1},\rho_{t+1}
\right),
]

rather than Wolfram’s simpler combinatorial form

[
E_t\rightarrow E_{t+1}.
]

This makes the edge state as fundamental as the edge topology.

The closest characterization is therefore:

> **A quantum-channel extension of a Wolfram-style hypergraph model, in which spatial adjacency, entanglement, and gravitational field state are encoded by dynamically evolving, orthogonal edge channels.**

The model is Wolfram-like in ontology, but its identification of hyperedges with entangled quantum channels—and gravity with their collective excitation—is a substantive additional hypothesis.
