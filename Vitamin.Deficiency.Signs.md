<b>Q: What are signs of deficiency in each of the major vitamins?</b>

**A:** Vitamin deficiencies often develop gradually and many symptoms overlap. A single symptom rarely identifies a specific deficiency, but characteristic patterns can be suggestive.

| Vitamin                           | Common signs of deficiency                                            | Severe or advanced deficiency                                       |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vitamin A (Retinol)**           | Dry skin, dry eyes, poor night vision, frequent infections            | Night blindness, corneal damage (xerophthalmia), blindness          |
| **Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)**         | Fatigue, irritability, poor concentration, reduced appetite           | Beriberi (heart failure or neuropathy), Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome |
| **Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)**       | Cracks at corners of mouth, sore throat, swollen tongue, dry lips     | Eye inflammation, anemia, skin disorders                            |
| **Vitamin B3 (Niacin)**           | Fatigue, skin sensitivity, digestive upset                            | Pellagra: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia ("the three Ds")           |
| **Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)** | Rare; fatigue, headache, numbness or burning feet                     | Neurologic symptoms (rare)                                          |
| **Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)**       | Cracked lips, swollen tongue, depression, numbness, weakened immunity | Peripheral neuropathy, seizures, anemia                             |
| **Vitamin B7 (Biotin)**           | Hair thinning, brittle nails, skin rash around nose and mouth         | Depression, tingling, muscle pain                                   |
| **Vitamin B9 (Folate)**           | Fatigue, weakness, pale skin, mouth sores                             | Megaloblastic anemia; fetal neural tube defects during pregnancy    |
| **Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)**       | Fatigue, numb hands and feet, balance problems, memory issues         | Permanent nerve damage, megaloblastic anemia                        |
| **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)**     | Easy bruising, bleeding gums, slow wound healing, fatigue             | Scurvy: loose teeth, joint pain, poor healing                       |
| **Vitamin D**                     | Muscle weakness, bone pain, frequent fractures                        | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults)                           |
| **Vitamin E**                     | Rare; muscle weakness, poor coordination                              | Peripheral neuropathy, retinal damage, hemolytic anemia             |
| **Vitamin K**                     | Easy bruising, prolonged bleeding, frequent nosebleeds                | Serious hemorrhage; impaired blood clotting                         |

## Common symptom patterns

Certain symptoms narrow the possibilities:

| Symptom                  | Common deficiencies                             |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Fatigue                  | B1, B3, B6, B9, B12, C, D                       |
| Numbness or tingling     | B1, B6, B12, E                                  |
| Muscle weakness          | D, E, B1                                        |
| Poor night vision        | A                                               |
| Bleeding gums            | C, K                                            |
| Cracked mouth corners    | B2, B6                                          |
| Swollen or smooth tongue | B2, B6, B9, B12                                 |
| Hair loss                | Biotin (B7), zinc, iron (iron is not a vitamin) |
| Bone pain                | D, C (advanced)                                 |
| Frequent infections      | A, C, D, B6                                     |

## Deficiencies that are relatively common

In developed countries, these deficiencies are encountered most often:

1. Vitamin D
2. Vitamin B12 (particularly in older adults, vegetarians, and vegans)
3. Folate (B9)
4. Vitamin C (in people with very restricted diets)
5. Vitamin B6 (certain medications or alcohol use)

Deficiencies of vitamins A, E, and K are much less common because they are fat-soluble and the body stores them, although they can occur with disorders that impair fat absorption.

## Important caveat

Many deficiency symptoms overlap with thyroid disease, diabetes, anemia from iron deficiency, chronic infection, autoimmune disease, and medication side effects. Laboratory testing is generally required to confirm a vitamin deficiency before treatment, particularly for vitamins B12, D, and folate, since taking large doses unnecessarily can sometimes cause harm or mask another underlying condition.
