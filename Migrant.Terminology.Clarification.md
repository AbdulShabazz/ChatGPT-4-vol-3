<b>Q: I'm confused on terminology: are all migrants U.S. citizens?</b>

**A:** No. **“Migrant” does not imply U.S. citizenship.** It describes movement from one place to another, not a particular legal status.

In U.S. terminology:

| Term                               | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Migrant**                        | Broad term for a person who moves, domestically or internationally, often for work, family, safety, or other reasons.                                                         |
| **Immigrant**                      | Generally, someone who moves to the United States from another country intending to reside here. In U.S. immigration law, “immigrant” also has a more specific legal meaning. |
| **Lawful permanent resident**      | A non-citizen authorized to live permanently in the U.S.; commonly a **green-card holder**.                                                                                   |
| **Nonimmigrant**                   | A non-citizen admitted temporarily under a particular status, such as certain students, tourists, or temporary workers.                                                       |
| **Undocumented immigrant/migrant** | A non-citizen without currently valid authorization to remain in the U.S.                                                                                                     |
| **Naturalized citizen**            | Someone who was not a U.S. citizen at birth but subsequently acquired citizenship through naturalization.                                                                     |
| **U.S. citizen**                   | A person possessing U.S. citizenship, whether acquired at birth or later through naturalization.                                                                              |

So a person described as a **migrant in the United States could be a U.S. citizen, permanent resident, temporary visa holder, asylum seeker, refugee, or undocumented person**. “Migrant” alone tells you essentially nothing about citizenship status.

There is also an important distinction between **migrant** and **immigrant**: someone moving from Connecticut to Florida for seasonal work could reasonably be called a *migrant worker* while being a U.S. citizen their entire life.
